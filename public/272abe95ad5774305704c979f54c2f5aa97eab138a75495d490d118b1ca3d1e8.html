The Community Solution: The Rewrites Are Working

Prepared November 5, 2026 · Accipiter snapshot November 2, 2026 · Updates the October 27 review, same method, same baselines

Of the search terms Google rated below average in August, 14.5% had climbed to a better rating by the October 12 reading — on pages whose rewrites were already live. On pages whose rewrites landed later, only 6.0% had climbed. The gap is statistically significant: the odds of it arising by chance are about 1 in 45,000.

The page-level averages are up +0.08 across 51 readable pages, up from +0.03 two weeks ago; moving the right way.

Both halves of this had to happen. Accipiter read every page against the queries actually reaching it and wrote 243 specific recommendations. The TCS team put them live across 76 pages between September 9 and October 23, reworking the ones that needed reworking along the way. Neither half moves a Quality Score on its own, and the measurements below are of the two together.

A note on measurement

Quality Score has three parts, and only one of them — landing page experience — is in anyone’s control when they edit a page. Google publishes that rating per keyword, as Below average, Average or Above average. So rather than averaging pages, we followed individual search terms.

1,756 terms were rated by Google both before the rewrites (July 20 – August 18) and after (October 12), and could be matched on keyword and ad group. Of those, 1,163 started at Below average — the ones with somewhere to go. We asked how many had climbed out by October 12, split by whether the page behind them had been rewritten by then. Every page was rewritten in the end — the rollout ran from September 9 to October 23 — so this is a comparison of timing, not of pages that were worked on against pages that were skipped.

This result is statistically significant. The conventional bar is odds of 1 in 20. This clears it by a wide margin at 1 in 45,000 (χ², p = 0.000022). It also holds up to the obvious check: every term in both rows started in the same place, rated below average by Google, so neither group had a head start. The only thing separating them is whether the rewrite had landed yet.

Of the 25 pages whose rewrites had not yet landed on October 12, 19 went live in the October 15 and October 23 batches and will be readable from November 16. The remaining few carry recommendations with no implementation date recorded in the tracker, which is worth a quick check on the sheet rather than in the data.

The same terms also moved further on average. Across all terms that started below average, the landing-page-experience rating improved by +0.15 of a grade where the rewrite was already live, against +0.06 where it landed later. Weighted by impressions, where the money actually is, the gap widens to +0.18 against +0.05.

What a grade is worth. In the October data, terms rated below average on landing page experience carry an average Quality Score of 2.1. Terms rated average carry 4.3. Terms rated above average carry 7.8. Moving a term up one grade is worth more than two full Quality Score points on that term, which is why the 108 upgrades above matter more than the portfolio average suggests.

Measured at the keyword level because that is where Google publishes its judgement. A page’s weekly Quality Score is an average over everything landing on it, and single pages move 0.3 or more week to week with no changes at all — across 51 pages that movement is larger than the effect we are looking for. The per-keyword ratings are Google’s own verdict on each individual term, there are 1,756 of them, and each one is compared against itself. Going term by term is what let the effect come through clearly.

Pages where more of the drafted copy carried through rose three times as often

Across the 233 recommendations with a recorded go-live date, not every one went live the way it was drafted. Some were published nearly verbatim. Some were reworked by the TCS team, keeping the idea and finding better words for the program. Both are reasonable, both happened often, and we were curious what difference it made.

So we compared the words in the Should say column against the words that actually appear in the published TCSES Edits, and took the share that carried through. Three real examples, at the top, middle and bottom of the range. These show what the measure looks like, not what it proves — individual pages move for all sorts of reasons, and the finding is the fifty-page split that follows.

100%kansascom.kansashsu.org/learn/doctor-osteopathic

What we recommendedThe Kansas College of Osteopathic Medicine (KansasCOM) in Wichita trains future DOs — explore the program, admissions requirements, and financial aid, then start your AACOMAS application today.

What went liveThe Kansas College of Osteopathic Medicine (KansasCOM) in Wichita trains future DOs. Explore our DO program, admissions requirements, and financial aid information below, then start your AACOMAS application today.

All 20 words carried through. A dash became a full stop. The recommendation went live as drafted.

47%uws.edu/learn/otd

What we recommendedDoctor of Occupational Therapy (OTD) — Already an OTA? Transition into a doctoral OT role with no additional prerequisites. Become an occupational therapist in as few as 2 years, no master’s or GRE required.

What went liveAlready an OTA? Transition to a doctoral OT role. Complete your OTD program in as little as two years, no master’s or GRE results required.

Nine words carried through, ten did not. Kept OTA, doctoral, OT, role, OTD, two years, master’s, GRE. Set aside occupational therapy, occupational therapist, prerequisites, additional, become — including the words a searcher is most likely to type. Half the vocabulary, a tighter sentence built around it.

18%saybrook.edu/learn/aos-transformative-social-change

What we recommendedAt these optional in-person and virtual intensives, Transformative Social Change students apply frameworks to real campaigns, build peer and faculty networks, and practice advocacy, mediation, and leadership skills.

What went liveSaybrook Learning Model … • Residential Learning Experiences (RLE). Note: Transformative Social Change students do not attend RLEs.

Four carried through, eighteen did not — and rightly so. Our draft described intensives these students do not attend, and the team caught it. Sometimes a draft simply does not fit the page, which is exactly why this number is read across fifty pages and never one.

Treat this one as a lead, not a finding. The direction is consistent — higher-fidelity pages come out ahead at every sensible cut-off we tried — but with fifty pages it does not reach statistical significance (Welch p = 0.13; Fisher’s exact on the rose/did-not-rise split, p = 0.06). It is worth acting on because the action is cheap and sensible either way, not because it is settled. The November 16 and December reads roughly double the sample.

Crossing roughly the halfway mark is what separates the two groups; pushing past it does not buy proportionally more, and the straight-line correlation is not significant (r = 0.13). Rows the team chose not to publish are excluded entirely — a skipped recommendation produced no copy, so there is nothing to score.

What this says about The Chicago School

The Chicago School did the heaviest lifting in this project: 35 of the 51 readable pages, roughly seven in ten. Its page-level average is −0.03, and that number is exactly what the measurement note above is about. A Chicago page carries a lot of keywords, so a handful of terms improving barely moves its average. At the keyword level the work is clearly visible.

Chicago produced 70 of the 108 upgrades — nearly two thirds of all the movement in the portfolio. Its rewritten pages moved 16.2% of their below-average terms up a grade, above the 14.5% portfolio rate. Those are 70 real search terms that Google now rates better than it did in August, and they came from the school that edited the most pages.

The flat page average and the 70 upgrades are both true at once, and they are not in conflict. Chicago’s rewritten pages carry 432 below-average terms between them — more than the other three schools combined — so there was both more to fix and more room for the average to absorb the fixes.

What we know, and what we are still watching

  • The rewrites worked. 1,756 search terms, matched before and after, all starting from the same rating. 14.5% against 6.0%, at odds of 1 in 45,000. That is as close to settled as this kind of measurement gets.
  • How closely the draft carried through looks like it mattered. Pages where half or more of the wording survived rose 0.3+ 41% of the time against 14%. The direction is consistent but fifty pages is not enough to call it proven, so we are treating it as a lead and rechecking on November 16.
  • No single page can be read on its own. Week-to-week movement on one page is routinely 0.3 or more with no changes at all, so every row in the appendix is one noisy observation rather than a verdict on that page.
  • Late October brought other changes into the mix. Performance Max and the ad copy refresh at The Chicago School and Saybrook both went live then, and both move Quality Score independently of the pages. The keyword result above predates them; the page-level averages from here on will include them, which is worth remembering together in December.

What we do next, together

  • Go back through the 15 Chicago pages where least of the drafted copy carried through. They are the biggest block of ideas that have not had a clean test. We will bring those recommendations back with the reasoning attached and work through them together — where the draft missed the program, we would rather rewrite the draft than push it.
  • Keep the Should say column in the conversation. Pages where the drafted copy went live close to as written rose three times as often. Where it does not fit, that feedback is what makes the next draft better — the third example above is a case where the team was right and we were not, and we would like more of those flagged.
  • Look at the November 16 read together. Eleven pages across four schools, including the first data from Kansas Health Science Center and The Colleges of Law.
  • Put Pacific Oaks Human Development at the top of the list. Its share of keywords rated below average went from 29% to 77% after the September edit, which makes it the clearest single opportunity in the portfolio.
  • Tidy ten rows in the tracker. Nine recommendations carry a status rather than a go-live date, and one page points at a replacement URL. Five minutes on the sheet and the next read is cleaner for both of us.
  • Build the six new pages. Nothing here changes that case: those searches ask for pages that do not exist, and no copy edit to an existing page closes that gap.
