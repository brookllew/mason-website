Saybrook University Google Ads: What Changed Since Q4 2025

Google Ads performance for Saybrook University, January 2024 to September 2026. Prepared by Mason Interactive for The Community Solution Education System.

Reported Google Ads conversions for Saybrook fell from about 1,470 a month in Q4 2025 to about 600 in July to September 2026. Most of that drop is not lost demand. Performance Max supplied half of Q4 2025's reported conversions at about $13 each and was scaled back in January 2026; phone calls stopped counting as conversions in April; and mind-body medicine (M.B.M.) has been counted at the Salesforce "Contacted" stage, not the inquiry form, since April. Inquiry forms from the search programs fell about 10% on 4% more spend, and admissions contacted slightly more of them. What did change is cost: budget was directed from M.B.M., the lowest-cost program, to programs that cost $425 to $590 an inquiry, and search clicks have cost about 30% more since July. The larger step-down came earlier: in June 2025, when search ads moved to new landing pages, the share of search clicks that became inquiries fell by about 40%, and it has not recovered.

Reading it. Three things overlap. Counting changed several times, which moves reported numbers on its own. Conversion rate stepped down in mid-2025 and stayed down. And in 2026 budget was reprioritized from the lowest-cost program toward higher-cost ones while clicks got more expensive. The theses below take each piece in turn.

Reading it. Program priorities were set on enrollment outcomes: future-start deposits and how inquiries become students. That is the right basis for choosing programs, and it has a cost in inquiry volume, because the programs prioritized run at two to three times M.B.M.'s cost per inquiry.

Two decisions stand apart from program priorities. The move to the new landing pages changed how many clicks became inquiries in every degree program, and turning off Performance Max removed about half of the reported total. Together they account for most of the difference between the numbers reported in 2025 and those reported now.

The peak. Performance Max reported 1,345 inquiries in December 2025 on $15.8K, and 1,113 in January 2026 on $2.6K, about $2 each. In both months that was more than every search program combined. In February it reported 15, and it has reported 15 to 70 a month since.

Seventeen steady months, then a break. From January 2024 through May 2025, 7 to 8% of search clicks became an inquiry. In June 2025 it was 5.3%, and it has been about 4% since. Inquiry forms from search fell from about 1,380 a month in January to May 2025 to about 820 in June to December 2025, on similar spend and the same click cost.

The timing follows the pages, program by program. Search ads moved from the degrees.saybrook.edu landing pages to the saybrook.edu/learn program pages on May 28, 2025, for M.B.M. and on June 5 for the other programs. M.B.M.'s rate fell the week it moved, while the programs still on the earlier pages held. Those programs fell the week they moved.

It is not the searches, the click cost or the counter. Degree searches were 43% of visible search spend in March to May 2025 and 41% in June to August, and the fall shows up in every category of non-brand search. Click cost was flat. Both inquiry counters show the same drop.

The new brand page converts better. Brand searches went from 7.3% to 10.7% on their new page, so the issue is specific to the program pages.

What to check. The inquiry form on the saybrook.edu/learn program pages against the earlier landing pages (length, position on the page, load time on a phone); Salesforce inquiry counts for May and June 2025, to confirm fewer inquiries arrived; and a test that sends part of one program's traffic to a page built like the earlier ones. This is the largest single change in the account, and it predates Q4 2025.

"Before" and "after" are measured from each program's own move date. The one-week window when only M.B.M. had moved is a small sample (about 70 M.B.M. inquiry forms), but it reads the same on both counters.

What was wrong. On the M.B.M. landing page, the inquiry form sat far down the page on a phone and returned an error on submit until the degree and start term were selected a second time. Brand spend fell, but not because budget ran short. The campaign lost almost no brand searches to budget in 2026 and showed on a larger share of them than in Q4 2025. Spend fell because each click cost less and there were fewer searches to show on.

The searches themselves declined. Clicks on the exact search "saybrook university" ran at about 1,050 to 1,100 a month in April to June and fell to 823 in July, 765 in August and 642 in September. Brand clicks in September were 37% below September 2025.

Never above 3.5. Saybrook's Quality Score has sat between 2.2 and 3.5 out of 10 in every month since January 2024. Brand keywords score about 8; program keywords score 2.5 to 3.

Landing pages got better. Almost all spend was on keywords with a below-average landing page rating through mid-2025. That fell to about 40% in early 2026, after the move to the saybrook.edu/learn pages, and it is the reason Quality Score reached its best level then. Google rates the new pages higher even though fewer visitors complete the form on them (Thesis V).

Ad relevance went the other way. Below-average ad relevance grew from about a quarter of spend to nearly two-thirds. It is worst on Integrative and Functional Nutrition (81% of spend), the program that now receives the most budget.

A low Quality Score means paying more for the same position, so each point recovered lowers click cost on every program.

Thesis IX: The Ad Copy Lost Its Calls to Action

Share of non-brand search impressions from ads with at least one call-to-action headline (apply, enroll, request info, get started, start in, today, deadline, sign up).

From about 70% to about 30%. In 2024, about 72% of non-brand search impressions came from ads that asked for something. That fell to 48% in January to May 2025, 42% in Q4 2025 and 30% in January to July 2026. A rebuild in August lifted it to 47% for the month.

Google marked the ads down. Ad relevance, one of the three parts of Quality Score, fell over the same period (Thesis VIII).

The match-type shift. Phrase match carried 83 to 98% of keyword spend until August 2024. Broad match took over that autumn and reached 86% in July 2025. Since the March 2026 rebuild it has run at 53 to 63%.

AI Max is growing. Google's keywordless matching took about 4% of search spend in August 2025, about 12% in early 2026 and about 23% in July to September 2026.

What people search has barely moved. Degree, program and school searches have been 42 to 44% of visible search spend in every period, under phrase match in 2024 and under broad match and AI Max now. Certificate, course and how-to searches were about a third of spend in mid-2024, when 7 to 8% of clicks became inquiries, and are about 30% now. The change in match types did not change the mix of searches, so it does not explain the lower conversion rate (Thesis V).
