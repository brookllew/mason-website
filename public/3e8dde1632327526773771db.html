Troubadour: Seven Ads, One Message

Prepared November 13, 2026 · From Meta’s public Ad Library, ads active in the United States on November 13 · No access to your account was used

You just raised £3m to grow in North America. Right now, an American scrolling Instagram can see seven Troubadour ads, all launched this week, and all saying the same thing: 20% off the Seasonal Edit.

The work is beautiful. The bags look exactly as premium as they are. But to Meta’s delivery system, seven variations of one idea look a lot like one ad, and a discount is the only reason the ads give to buy. Below is what we see, what has worked for the bag brands we have run for years, and the five ads we would make first.

What we see when we watch them

  1. One concept, seven cuts. The same editorial look runs through all seven: a bag on a plain backdrop or a model walking a European street, the same small Troubadour wordmark, and the same lime “20% off” overlay. Since Meta’s Andromeda update, ads this similar compete for the same people instead of finding new ones. Creative variety is now how the system finds new audiences.
  2. The discount is doing all the selling. Nothing in the ads says why a Troubadour bag is worth what it costs at full price. American shoppers who meet the brand through a sale learn to wait for the next one. Promotions work best on top of a full-price layer of ads that runs all year, and nothing older than November 10 is running.
  3. The product never gets to work. The copy promises “ergonomic design and smart organization,” but no ad opens the bag. Nobody packs it, slides in a laptop, or walks through rain with it. For a bag priced as an investment, the inside is the argument.
  4. The offer is hard to read on a phone. The overlay is thin lime text sitting mid-frame over light backgrounds. With the sound off, in a fast feed, most people will see a nice bag and miss both the offer and the name.
  5. Nothing speaks to America yet. The settings, the voice, and the absence of reviews all read as a London brand speaking to London. That is a strength to lean on (“designed in London”), but the US buyer still needs to see someone like them carrying it.
The opportunity, plainly: you already have the hardest part, a premium product and a brand people want to be seen with. What is missing is range. A small set of genuinely different ads gives Meta more ways to find American buyers and gives those buyers a reason to pay full price.

The five ads we would make first

1 · Demonstration

Pack the Pioneer, live

15 seconds, one take: laptop, charger, water bottle, jacket, and it still zips flat. The bag opens in the first two seconds.

Shows the “smart organization” the copy already promises. An open bag answers the price question before anyone asks it.

2 · Social proof

Reviews in the frame

An image or carousel built around one real customer line and the star rating, at full price, with no discount.

Proof that is not a price. This becomes the full-price layer that keeps running after the sale ends.

3 · Founders

Two founders, one bag

Samuel and Abel, 20 seconds, on why they started Troubadour and what they refused to compromise on.

Premium buyers buy the story behind the price, and nobody can copy it.

4 · American creator

A day on a US commute

A creator’s point of view, filmed on a phone: subway, office, dinner, with one bag the whole way.

Puts the bag in an American life. It looks native to the feed, which polished brand film does not.

5 · Comparison

The bag that replaces three

A work tote, a gym bag and a weekender, swapped for one Troubadour, shown side by side.

Reframes the price as consolidation, not indulgence.

Fix today · no new shoot

Make the offer readable

Move the overlay to the top third, set it larger on a dark band, and burn in captions on every video.

The same footage, readable with the sound off. This one change costs an afternoon.
