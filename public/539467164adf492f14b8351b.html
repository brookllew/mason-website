The Chicago School Google Ads: What Changed Since Q4 2025

Google Ads performance for The Chicago School, January 2024 to September 2026. Prepared by Mason Interactive for The Community Solution Education System.

To follow The Community Solution's move to a One University model, the account was rebuilt in mid-2025. The campus pages, the best-converting pages in the account, went away, along with most YouTube, and broad-match keywords were added for volume. Quality Score fell, but Q4 2025 volume held on higher spend, broad matching and ACP inquiries that could not enroll. In 2026, The Community Solution narrowed ACP for licensure and moved budget to higher-cost priority programs, while the brand campaign was underfunded, YouTube was paused, and ad copy had lost most of its calls to action. Each of those raised the cost of an inquiry; together they cut volume by about 40%.

Reading it. Two phases. In June and July 2025 the account was restructured in about six weeks to follow The Community Solution's One University model: campus campaigns and most YouTube paused, conversion goals reset, new keyword sets and broad-match terms added, and a YouTube Subscriptions campaign launched. Volume rose, but Quality Score fell and low-intent inquiries came in. From late January 2026 a second set of decisions pulled volume back: YouTube paused, ACP narrowed and cut, the brand name blocked from program campaigns in May, and broad-match keywords paused from July, while click costs rose. The theses below take each piece in turn.

Thesis I: YouTube Reach Made Search Money Work Harder

The pattern. YouTube reach has fallen for two years, from about 5.9M impressions a month to 1.3M. Cost per inquiry held between $209 and $225 while reach stayed above roughly 2.5M, then rose 36% to $307 once it fell below that, on the same monthly spend.

The subscriptions campaign mattered for reach, not inquiries. It bought about 800K impressions a month for $3K to $5K. Pausing it at the end of January 2026 took YouTube reach to near zero in February and March.

Search got more expensive at the same time. Average search CPC was about $12 through 2024 and 2025 and about $14.70 in 2026, consistent with search doing more of the work alone.

What this does not prove. The Psy.D. ACP budget was also cut in February 2026, so two changes landed in the same month. Month to month the link is noisy because of seasonality; the period averages are where it shows clearly.

How to test it. Restore YouTube reach to 2.5M+ impressions a month in selected markets and hold it off in comparable ones for six to eight weeks, then compare search cost per inquiry. The account has run geo holdout tests before (Austin, Charlotte), so the setup is familiar.

Thesis IV: Search Clicks Got 40 to 60% More Expensive From May 2026

Average cost per click on the largest program pages and across all program search, January 2024 to September 2026.

Two years of stability, then a break. Program search CPC held between about $10.70 and $14 from January 2024 to April 2026. From May 2026 it rose to $16 to $18.50. CMHC went from about $14 to $16 to between $20 and $25; ABA from $7 to $10 to about $14.

The account also stopped showing up as often. Program search impression share was 26 to 34% through 2024, with about 45% of eligible searches lost to ad rank. It was 25 to 31% in the first half of 2025, with rank losses rising to 50 to 65%. It then fell to 16 to 17% after the July 2025 restructure, and reached 12.5% in February to April 2026, when 70 to 77% of eligible searches were lost to ad rank. It recovered to 18 to 22% in the summer only as bids and costs rose.

Same pages, higher prices. The landing pages did not change, and impressions for program search fell about 27% between Q4 2025 and now on similar spend. Share of auctions lost to ad rank rose again in August and September after bids were raised in the spring.

Not Quality Score, and not the brand exclusion. Quality Score improved in May 2026 (Thesis IX). The search terms reports show program campaigns spent only $3K to $15K a month on searches containing the university's name, and kept getting about 40 of those inquiries a month after the May 8 negative; the block only bit in September. That is too small to move the average cost per click. The rise lines up instead with the spring bid and budget pushes on higher-cost programs (Theses III and X) and, from July, the broad-match pullback (VIII).

Thesis V: Conversion Rates Are Down, and It Is Not a Tracking Change

Inquiry form conversions in Google Ads against GA4's own generate_lead count for Google Ads traffic, February 2024 to September 2026.

Two independent counts move together. GA4 and Google Ads use different tags, yet both fall by a similar amount through 2026: GA4 from about 3,900 in January to about 2,500 in September (paced), Google Ads from about 3,540 to about 2,220. A tracking fault would usually show up in one and not the other.

Program search conversion rate. About 6 to 7% in summer 2024, 5 to 6% through 2025, and about 4 to 5.4% in 2026, with the lowest months in February to April and August to September.

The Community Solution sees it across all channels (Feb. 10 and April 14 meetings), which points to something beyond Google Ads: inquiry-form changes, the new brand, or shifts in who is searching. Their analyst's findings would help close this out.

Thesis VII: Brand Searches Went Uncovered Because the Brand Campaign Ran Out of Budget

Monthly impressions on brand search ads, thousands, January 2024 to September 2026 (top). Brand campaign impression share and share lost to budget, January 2024 to September 2026 (bottom).

Coverage fell, not only demand. The brand campaign showed on 76 to 85% of eligible brand searches through 2024 and 75 to 82% in the first half of 2025. From July 2025 that fell to 63 to 67%, and it hit 58% in August 2026. In the weakest months the brand campaign lost 22 to 31% of brand searches to budget: it ran out of money before the day ended.

What happened to the budget. The brand campaign ran at up to $5,000 a day in late April 2026. On May 21 to 22 its budget was cut to $1,700 a day (May 21 to 22), then to as low as $400 to $600 a day in July. Brand inquiries fell from about 560 in May to about 390 in June, 350 in July and 320 in August, at roughly $100 each, the cheapest inquiries in the account.

Why it matters. Brand searches are the cheapest and best-converting inquiries in the account, about 7% conversion. The search terms reports confirm the loss was in the brand campaign itself: its visible inquiries fell from about 455 in May to about 255 in August, while brand searches picked up by program campaigns stayed small (about 40 a month).

The fix is quick. When the brand budget was raised in mid-September 2026, impression share went back to 74% for the month. Keeping the brand campaign fully funded is the lowest-cost inquiry source available.

Still open. Whether underlying brand demand also softened; Google Trends or Search Console brand query data would show it.

Thesis VIII: AI Max Opened the Funnel in 2025, and Closing It in June 2026 Raised Costs

Program search impressions (bars, millions) and click-through rate (line), January 2024 to September 2026.

Click-through rate has been sliding for two years. Program search CTR ran 4.3 to 5.2% in the first half of 2024, fell to about 3.6 to 3.7% by late 2024, and has sat between about 3.1 and 4.1% since. September 2026 was the lowest month, at 3.1%. Falling CTR while impressions hold or rise is the pattern the industry associates with AI answers in search results pushing ads down the page.

But this is not what broke in 2026. CTR was actually higher in early 2026 (3.9 to 4.1%) than through most of 2025. It is a structural drag on the whole period, not the cause of the drop since February.

The timeline. Change History shows AI Max first turned on in June 2025 (Conversational AI) and on National General Search Terms in November 2025, and turned off on several test and licensure campaigns in December 2025 and on B.A. Psychology in April 2026. The ABA volume surge came mainly from broad-match keywords added by hand between June 26 and July 29, 2025, including RBT terms, rather than an AI Max switch. The pullback to traditional search came from July to September 2026, when about 50 broad-match keywords were paused. AI Max was tested again with AI Brief guardrails on four campaigns from August 13 and switched off on three of them by September 18.

What the data shows. Inquiries from the main ABA campaign (AOS Behavioral Sciences, Behavior Analysis) went from about 310 in Q1 2025 and 400 in Q2 to about 1,550 in Q3 and 1,720 in Q4 2025, then 1,160 in Q1 2026, 1,300 in Q2, and about 810 in Q3 through September 26. Part of the 2025 jump is older ABA campaigns being folded into this one: inquiries to the ABA landing page across all campaigns did not rise over the same period.

The link to cost. Program search cost per click averaged $12 to $13.50 a quarter through Q1 2026, then $15.60 in Q2 and $17.50 in Q3 2026. The rise started in May, before broad match was paused, so the broad-match pullback is not the first cause; the May 8 brand exclusion (Thesis IV) is a better fit for the timing, with the July to September narrowing adding to it.

The trade-off for planning. Industry results for AI Max are mixed: Google reports about 7% more conversions at similar cost, while an independent study of 250+ accounts found a median 13% uplift with a 16% higher CPA, and some inquiry-generation advertisers saw volume rise while inquiry quality fell. For The Chicago School, the 2025 run showed both sides: more platform inquiries and more inquiries that could not become students. Any return to broader matching should be tracked separately so admissions can score those inquiries.

A deadline to know about. Google is automatically upgrading campaigns that use Dynamic Search Ads, automatically created assets or campaign-level broad match to AI Max, starting September 2026. Worth checking which Chicago School campaigns qualify so the change happens on our terms.

Thesis IX: Quality Score Broke in August 2025 and Has Only Partly Recovered

Average historical Quality Score across search keywords, weighted by impressions (line, left axis), and share of search spend on keywords with below-average landing page experience (bars, right axis). January 2024 to September 2026; February 2024 not pulled.

Stable and low through early 2025. Quality Score held between 3.0 and 3.5 out of 10 from January 2024 to March 2025, with about half of spend on keywords rated below average for landing page experience.

The break. The slide started in spring 2025 and broke in August 2025, the month campus-page spend was cut from about $80K to about $20K and the number of active keywords fell by a third, from about 2,700 to 1,770. Quality Score fell to 2.73 that month and kept falling to 2.46 by December, with 71 to 73% of spend on below-average landing pages.

The Q4 2025 peak happened at the worst Quality Score in the period. Volume was held up by the highest spend of the year and by AI Max's broader matching, not by account health.

Partial recovery in 2026. The rebuild started in January 2026, when active keywords jumped from about 1,800 to about 3,050 and Quality Score rose from 2.46 to 2.72, before the February changes. It has held between 2.7 and 3.15 since. It is not what caused the February 2026 rank loss or the May 2026 rise in cost per click: Quality Score improved in both periods.

Direct response gave way to brand language. In October 2024 almost every non-brand search ad carried a call to action. By October 2025 only about half did, and it has stayed there. Recommendation. Every non-brand ad should carry at least two call-to-action headlines and one call-to-action description, with the program name and the search term close to the front, tested against current copy from October 1.
