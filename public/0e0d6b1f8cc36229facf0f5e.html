The Chicago School: phone calls as leads

Phone calls are the one lead source that never reaches Salesforce. Mason has tracked calls from paid search since 2013, first in Marchex and since 2020 in CallRail, and has now matched a decade of callers to Salesforce leads. The pattern is clear: callers who stayed on the line 5 minutes or more applied at 22%, more than double the 9% of shorter calls. In 2026 about 95 calls a month run that long, and none of them are counted as leads or attributed to the campaign that drove them.

Which calls are leads? Five minutes is the line

Callers who applied, by longest call

9,852 unique callers to Chicago School tracking numbers, 2014–2017, matched by phone number to Salesforce leads with an application received.

Why 5 minutes, not 10 or 20

  • The jump happens at 5 minutes. Application rates sit around 8–11% for calls under 5 minutes, then nearly double to 20% for 5–10 minute calls.
  • 10 minutes adds little precision. Callers at 10+ minutes applied at 25%, versus 22% at 5+ minutes.
  • But it loses half the applicants. A 5-minute line captures 477 of the 1,205 callers who applied; a 10-minute line captures 281.
  • 20+ minutes is a strong signal, not a sure one. 28% of those callers applied.
  • This is the same line Mason proposed in July 2014: count unique calls over 5 minutes as leads.

What this data can and can't tell us

  • Matching is by phone number only. We compare the number a caller dialed from with the home phone on the Salesforce record. If someone calls from a spouse's, family or work phone, or from a number that isn't on their record, the call won't match. Every rate here is a floor.
  • Applications, not enrollments. "Started Class" is filled in for only 641 students in the export, nearly all 2014 terms, too few to measure. Enrollment data from Salesforce would finish the picture.
  • Call length includes hold and menu time. The 2022 call review found callers stuck in a menu loop for about 13 minutes. Long calls should be checked against recordings, and talk time used where available.
  • Paid search only. Tracking numbers show to paid-search visitors. Callers from organic search, email or direct visits dial the main admissions number and aren't counted.
  • Order isn't tested. A match means the caller also applied at some point, before or after the call.

What we recommend

  • Count answered calls of 5+ minutes as phone leads and send them to Salesforce with campaign, keyword and landing page, so they show up in source attribution next to form leads.
  • Match callers to Salesforce leads every month by phone number, and share starts or enrollments so we can measure call-to-enrollment directly.
  • Fix the queue and voicemail. By October, 27% of weekday business-hours calls ended in the hold queue or at voicemail, up from 11% in June. Weekend callers almost never reach a person, and the voicemail box sometimes fails outright (see the appendix).
  • Add tracking for Saybrook, Pacific Oaks and Colleges of Law; today their phone demand is invisible.
  • Add a tracking number for organic/SEO visitors, so all click-to-calls are counted, not just paid.
