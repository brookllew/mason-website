How Accipiter Works

Every Day, Every Week, Every Month

Accipiter runs Edward Sexton's Meta and Google Ads accounts on a fixed cadence. Every day it forecasts the month and flags what needs attention. Every weekday it runs a different check on each platform: performance and keywords on Monday, audiences and ad copy on Tuesday, creative and ad groups on Wednesday, Meta ad copy on Thursday and negative keywords on Friday. Every month it runs a five-week rotation of deeper Google reviews and a creative report for Meta. Alongside the cadence it runs longer projects, such as the landing page quality review. Each check below shows what it does, what it found and what we are doing about it.

Daily

MetaForecast

What it does: projects month-end spend and results by campaign and ad.

What it shows: spend on track for the £2,000 goal; one purchase (£493, remarketing) so far. Acquisition has spent £641 and traffic £239, neither with a purchase. Its analysis recommends moving about £550 into remarketing and checking the tracking.

Our view: see step 1. Remarketing takes more budget once its audience is wider. The new ads alone have recorded three add-to-carts and two started checkouts this month, so tracking is firing.

GoogleForecast

What it does: projects month-end Google spend and results against the £2,000 goal (about $2,670).

What it shows: $1,100 spent, heading to about $2,000. New York and Los Angeles brand search: $247, 3 orders, $2,700, 22% click rate. National brand search: $359, no orders. Performance Max: $299, no orders. Its analysis says hold Performance Max and keep the competitor campaign capped.

Our view: step 5. The competitor campaign has been paused since Nov. 10. Budgets on brand search and Performance Max went up on Nov. 11, so the second half of the month will spend closer to goal.

MetaRecommendations

What it does: a daily pacing check with advisory budget moves.

What it shows: on pace; flags acquisition, traffic and remarketing as having no purchase in seven days, "monitor or reduce."

Our view: fair as a flag. Seven days is short for purchases at this price point.

Weekly

MetaMonday: Performance Review

What it does: compares the past week with the week before.

What it shows: week of Nov. 10: £418 spent, clicks up 65% to 2,483, cost per click down 32% to 17 pence, no purchases. It reads this as a possible tracking or checkout fault and suggests a test purchase.

Our view: the jump in clicks comes from the new traffic campaign, which is built to buy clicks, not purchases, so no purchases that week is not proof of a fault. A test purchase is cheap insurance, and we will run one.

GoogleMonday: Add Keyterms

What it does: suggests new keywords, from Google and from searches that already produced orders.

What it shows: Accipiter suggests "edward sexton" as an exact keyword (2 orders, $116 each, in 30 days) and "edward sexton shirt" (1 order).

Our view: step 7.

MetaTuesday: Audience Analysis

What it does: shows which audiences the spend actually reached, and how they performed.

What it shows: over 30 days, the 3% lookalike of past purchasers took £1,165 with no purchase. Remarketing to past purchasers and recent add-to-carts took £395 and produced three purchases and £6,171. A 30-day website visitors audience exists but has no spend. All three purchases came from men aged 35 to 44.

Our view: this is the evidence for step 1.

GoogleTuesday: Ad Copy

What it does: checks the health of the search ads: how many each ad group has, how old they are and which are underperforming.

What it shows: every brand ad group runs a single ad, and none is new in 30 days. The main national brand ad has spent $587 in 30 days with no order.

Our view: step 4. See the proposed Google search ads.

MetaWednesday: Creative Review

What it does: tracks every ad's click rate over its life to spot fatigue.

What it shows: winners include the September Fall Collection image. Fatigued, and already retired: summer sale statics and carousels, the black tie launch video and several April images, each down 57% to 100% from their peak. Under-funded: the garden video (14.7% click rate) and the office video (7.2%).

Our view: agree. None of the fatigued ads is live; every live Meta ad is October or November creative. We recommend testing the garden and office videos in acquisition as well as traffic.

GoogleWednesday: Ad Group Breakout

What it does: looks for ad groups catching too many unrelated searches that should be split.

What it shows: scanned all eight search ad groups on Nov. 16; none flagged.

MetaThursday: Ad Copy Analysis

What it does: groups ads by their text and compares results.

What it shows: in 30 days, the only text pairing with purchases was "Shop the Autumn Collection" and "Edward Sexton Autumn Winter Tailoring now available." with Shop Now. "Signature Tailoring" took the most spend, £819, with none. Learn More buttons drew clicks but no sales.

Our view: step 2. We will judge the swap on the purchases it brings, starting this week.

GoogleFriday: Add Negatives, with the Week One AI Negatives Review

What it does: two queues of searches to exclude. One is pure arithmetic on spend without results; the other is AI that checks searches against what Edward Sexton actually sells, with evidence.

What it shows: the arithmetic queue suggests "edward saxton" and "edward sexton sale." The AI queue suggests eight competitor names account-wide. Already added: Albert Thurston braces, Suitsupply, Armani suits, Prince of Wales check and Edwards Heavies; 64 suggestions dismissed after review.

Our view: dismiss the first two, which are people looking for Edward Sexton. For the competitor names, see step 6.

Monthly

MetaCreative Report (October)

What it does: a frozen month-end read of every creative, ranked by click rate.

What it shows: the images that earn the most clicks show one full garment on the model, on a plain or textured ground, with little text: the September Fall Collection images (1.9% to 2.0%) and the October red suit. Cropped and abstract images did worst: the over-the-shoulder dinner jacket (0.38%) and the shirt portrait (0.15%). The tweed suit image produced two purchases worth £5,678.

Our view: a useful brief for the next shoot. Its October revenue total includes the £7,424 trunk-show order, which Edward Sexton has told us did not come from the ads.

GoogleWeek Two: Keyword Relevance

What it does: checks the top keywords by spend against what the client offers.

What it shows: 22% of top-keyword spend ($355 of $1,648) went to competitor names, all in the competitor campaign. The biggest single keyword, the broad "edward london clothing," spent $479 with no order.

Our view: the competitor campaign was paused on Nov. 10 and "edward london clothing" on Nov. 16.

GoogleWeek Three: Dead Keyterms

What it does: finds searches that used to produce orders and stopped.

What it shows: in the national brand campaign, "edward sexton sale" went from 4 orders to none and "edward sexton" from 3 to none, while its impressions fell from 3,984 to 468.

Our view: those searches did not die; since the Sept. 24 split, they are served by the New York and Los Angeles campaign, where they convert.

GoogleWeek Four: Landing Page Analysis

What it does: conversion rate by landing page over 30 days.

What it shows: the homepage took 537 clicks and 5 orders (0.93%). Suits, jackets and trousers pages took 32 clicks between them, with none.

Our view: too few clicks on the collection pages to judge them on orders. Accipiter's page-by-page audit is under Landing Pages.

GoogleWeek Five: Geo Analysis, and Query Overlap

What they do: conversion rate by region, and searches served by more than one campaign at once.

What they show: England: 456 clicks in 30 days, no order. New York: 115 clicks, 1.5%. Overlap flags "edward sexton" served across three ad groups ($632) and a tuxedo cluster ($236, no order).

Our view: this supports sending tuxedo searches to their own page (step 8). It is also a question for Edward Sexton: whether UK online buyers simply buy in the shop, which better appointment tracking would show.

Projects

GoogleLanding Page Quality Review, Ongoing Since August

What it does: Accipiter's agents watch Google's Quality Score for every page the ads send people to, week by week, and write a test plan for each page on their own.

What it shows: the score for keywords landing on the homepage climbed from 7.0 in August to 9.5 on Nov. 16, with no keyword rated below average. The jump came from cleaning up which searches the ads answer: on Nov. 10 Accipiter's negative-keyword agent wrote its exclusions straight into the Google Ads account. Its agents also caught coat, tuxedo and trouser searches landing on the wrong pages, and drafted the routing and the page tests now in this plan.

Our view: this is the practice working as designed: the agent finds the gap, our team approves the fix, and the score moves. The page suggestions are steps 8 and 9 and the Landing Pages section.

In traffic, the new images draw twice the click rate of the images they joined (1.31% against 0.63%), led by the shirts image at 1.70%. Video still leads the format: the garden and office videos average 9.9%.

In acquisition, the new ads trail the older ones so far (0.60% against 0.86%), pulled down by the campaign image with overlay at 0.38%. The new shirts carousel, at 1.34%, beats every older acquisition ad.

In remarketing, the new ads lead (2.11% against 0.77%), on small numbers while the audience stays narrow.

Too early to judge on purchases. At this year's Meta cost per purchase of about £337, £101 of spend would not be expected to produce a purchase yet. The fair early test is clicks, site visits and add-to-carts; give the November ads two more weeks before cutting any.

The shirts image is the best new traffic ad. 222 clicks and 155 site visits for £14, at 6 pence a click, about the same as the garden video.

The campaign image with overlay is the weakest new acquisition ad. It took the most spend, £42, at a 0.38% click rate, though it did start one checkout. If it stays under 0.5% next week, its budget should go to the shirts carousel, whose click rate is more than three times higher.

The garden video is still the engine. 1,826 clicks and 1,277 site visits this month, but no add-to-carts. It is doing its job of bringing new people to the site; whether they come back to buy depends on remarketing reaching them, which today it does not (step 1).

The new remarketing ads reached 28 to 64 people each. That is the audience problem again, not a creative one.

The text needs work. Nearly every November ad uses one of two texts. The main one, "Edward Sexton Savile Row, seasonal tailoring from Edward Sexton," names the brand twice and gives no reason to click. The pairing that has produced purchases, "Shop the Autumn Collection" with "Edward Sexton Autumn Winter Tailoring now available.," is not used on any November ad. Two paused versions of the shirts carousel are headed only "Edward Sexton" and link to the homepage rather than the shirts page; they will be corrected before the reworked shirts carousel goes live (steps 2 and 3).
