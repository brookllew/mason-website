The Colleges of Law Google Ads: What Changed Since Q4 2025

Google Ads performance for The Colleges of Law, August 2025 to September 2026. Prepared by Mason Interactive for The Community Solution Education System.

Reported Google Ads inquiries for The Colleges of Law averaged 153 a month in Q4 2025 and 121 a month in July to September 2026, a fall of 21% on 4% less spend. Cost per inquiry went from $172 to $210. Q4 2025 was not the high point. The first half of 2026 ran higher, at 168 and then 179 a month, with 238 in May, so the decline is a summer 2026 event. Three things changed. Search budget fell 19% as Performance Max grew from 5% to 21% of spend and the spring's extra Hybrid J.D. funding ended. Search clicks cost 12% more, almost all of it on searches for the school's own name. And the pool of searches the account could appear for was about 45% smaller. Conversion rate held within half a point and no landing page moved, so this is a budget, cost and coverage story. September was the low month, at 93 inquiries, on the smallest Google budget of the period and with paid social paused for the awareness test. Paid social came back on Oct. 8.

The pool is about 45% smaller. Part of that is coverage we control: fewer keywords serving, the Ventura campaign's smaller area and the states excluded in September. Part is fewer people searching. The two cannot be separated from this data.

Mid-August to September looks different across the account. In those weeks, the window in the Oct. 6 review, more available impressions went unbought for budget (about 43%) than for ad rank (about 39%), because September carried the smallest budget of the period and the Hybrid J.D. campaigns lost about 80% of theirs to budget. Over the full quarter, ad rank is the larger loss. In either window the campus campaigns are limited by rank and Hybrid J.D. by budget.

Hybrid J.D. is limited by budget. Both Hybrid campaigns lose well over half of their available impressions to budget, and little to ad rank. Money converts directly into volume there.

The campus campaigns are limited by ad rank. The Santa Barbara and Ventura campaigns lose about 65% of available impressions to rank and little to budget. More budget alone adds little. Quality Score, landing pages and ad copy are the levers (Theses V and VI).

Where Google reports impression share only as "under 10%," 10% is used, so available impressions and budget losses for the Hybrid J.D. campaigns are minimums. Lost to budget is what remains after share won and share lost to rank.
